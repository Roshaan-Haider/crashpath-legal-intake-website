1. Why calls are recorded

CrashPath records telephone calls connecting to our campaign and intake lines for specific operational and quality management purposes:

  • Quality assurance & script adherence: Supervisors review recordings to ensure intake agents follow designated screening paths, maintain professional demeanor, and never offer unauthorized legal advice.
  • Agent coaching & calibration: Audio samples are reviewed in weekly team calibration sessions to ensure consistent factual capture across all shifts.
  • Verifiable record keeping: Accurate call audio protects both callers and participating law firms by maintaining an unaltered, timestamped record of initial claimant disclosures and consent affirmations.

2. Notice at call start

When an inbound call connects to a CrashPath campaign line, an automated disclosure or a live agent reads an explicit recording notice before any substantive intake screening begins.

Continuing the call after hearing the recording disclosure serves as consent to be recorded. Agents will not proceed with intake questions without providing this initial notice.

3. State consent differences

Telephone call recording rules vary significantly across US jurisdictions. In many states, recording is permissible with the consent of one party to the call. In other jurisdictions, state call-recording laws require all parties on the telephone line to give affirmative consent prior to recording.

Because CrashPath operates national intake queues connecting callers across various states, our default standard operates under strict notice protocols designed to ensure all participating parties are informed of recording status, regardless of originating area code.

4. If a caller declines

Callers maintain the right to decline audio recording at any time.

If you state that you do not consent to being recorded, the agent will acknowledge your preference, stop recording tools if technically supported on that line, or professionally explain that the campaign line requires recording for quality review. If recording cannot be disabled on an active queue, the agent will offer alternative contact methods, such as an unrecorded online submission or direct referral to an attorney office, before disconnecting the call.

5. Requesting copies

Callers or their authorized legal representatives may request a copy of their call audio recording or written verbatim transcript, subject to identity verification and data confidentiality guidelines.
